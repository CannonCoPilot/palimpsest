# The 1635 Rouen prelims against the 1609 Douai — a difference report

**Roadmap R6.4.** What the second edition of the Douay Old Testament **adds, drops and rewords** in the
Approbatio, the Preface and the apparatus. Every difference below is cited to a leaf in each edition. **No
difference is asserted from memory of the text**; each was read off the two rasters side by side.

## 0. What was compared, and on what

| | first edition | second edition |
|---|---|---|
| witness | **`OT1-1609-B`** — Boston Public Library, the base exemplar | **`OT-1635-M`** — the OT half of the 2007 Maximus Scriptorius reprint |
| imprint | Doway, **Laurence Kellam**, 1609 | Rouen, **Iohn Cousturier**, `M.DC.XXXV` |
| primary artefact | JP2 package (institutional capture) | the **PDF**'s embedded CCITT stencils |
| raster | continuous tone, **~545 ppi** (calibrated against a ruler leaf) | **1-bit, ~380 ppi**, letter-size re-lay-out |
| addressing | leaves as captured | package pages 0–2070, registered as a slice |

`OT-1635-M` was registered for this step. It is **not a witness to either OT tome's setting** and may never
supply an OT verse reading — it is a different edition. It is registered because **a difference cannot be
cited to a leaf in a witness that has no addresses.**

> **Why these differences are evidence and not noise.** The 1635 volume states on its own back matter
> (leaf 2070) that it reprints *"de l'edition de Laurens Kellam Imprimeur de Douay."* The Rouen house was
> therefore working *from* the Douai edition. **Every difference below is a deliberate editorial act**, not
> an independent transmission — which is what makes a difference report worth having at all.

## 1. Structure — the two editions correspond section for section

| section | 1609 (`OT1-1609-B`) | 1635 (`OT-1635-M`) |
|---|---|---|
| title page | leaf 11 | leaf 0 |
| **APPROBATIO** | leaf 12 | leaf 1 |
| Preface — *To the right welbeloved English reader* | leaves 13–24 (**12 pp.**) | leaves 2–11 (**10 pp.**) |
| The Summe and Partition of the Holy Bible | leaf 25 | leaf 12 |
| Proemial Annotations | leaves 26–28 | leaves 13–15 |
| The Summe of the Old Testament | leaf 27 (within) | leaf 14 (within) |
| Of Moyses the Author of the five first books | leaf 28 (within) | leaf 15 (within) |
| The Argument of the Book of Genesis | leaf 29 | leaf 16 |
| The Argument of Genesis | leaf 30 | leaf 17 |
| Genesis, chap. I | leaf 31 | leaf 18 |

**Nothing is added and nothing is dropped at section level.** Every section of the 1609 prelims is present
in the 1635, in the same order, and the 1635 introduces none of its own. The only length difference is the
**Preface, 12 leaves against 10** — and §3 shows that is a wider measure, not lost text.

## 2. The Approbatio — reprinted verbatim, including its date

**1609 leaf 12 · 1635 leaf 1.** The Latin is **word for word identical**, from *"NOS infrascripti, in alma
Duacensi vniuersitate…"* to the subscription.

**The 1635 retains the Douai subscription unchanged: `Duaci 8. Nouembris. 1609.`** The Rouen edition does
not re-approve itself; it reproduces the approbation of the edition it reprints, twenty-six years on. That
is consistent with the privilege's own claim to be reprinting Kellam's Douai edition, and it means **the
approbation dates the text, not the book.**

Differences are typographic only:

| | 1609 | 1635 |
|---|---|---|
| heading ornament | woodcut cartouche — heraldic shields, flanking figures | a plain typographic **fleuron band** |
| heading | `APPROBATIO.` set in a ruled panel | `APPROBATIO·` set open |
| measure | narrower; ~7 words a line | wider; ~9 words a line |
| punctuation | `Duaci. 8. Nouembris. 1609.` | `Duaci 8. Nouembris. 1609.` — the stop after *Duaci* absent |

## 3. The Preface — no passage added or dropped, resolved on the marginal apparatus

The Preface is the substantive document, and the question *"what did Rouen add or drop"* is answered
**without a word-by-word read of 22 pages** by collating its **marginal note sequence**. The notes are
short, numerous and content-bearing, so a passage added or removed would show up as a note without a
counterpart, localised to the leaf.

**All ~24 marginal notes correspond one to one, in the same order, across the whole Preface** —
1609 leaves 13–24 against 1635 leaves 2–11:

> delay in setting forth · why and how vulgar Scripture is allowed · Scriptures being hard · many take
> harm by reading · reading moderated · translated into divers tongues · a calumnious suggestion of
> Lutherans · what part is convenient for vulgar readers · why we translate the old Latin text · more pure
> than the Hebrew or Greek now extant · received by all Churches · turned into Greek · all others grown out
> of use / S. Hierom excelled · his Edition free from partiality · preferred before all others by Beza ·
> none yet in England allowed as sufficient · Preface before the New Testament · what is done in this
> Edition · divers readings resolved upon · they touched not present controversies · why some words are not
> translated · some Hebrew words not translated · more authority in sacred tongues · Protestants leave some
> words untranslated · corruptions in Protestant translations · against free will · against Melchisedech's
> sacrifice · against holy images · this Edition dedicated · Christ redeemed all · true faith first
> necessary · the twelve Apostles · pastoral cures · new doctrine falsely called the Gospel · grace more
> abundant · both wicked workers · innumerable saved · more happy that suffer persecution · English
> Catholics most happy in this age · confession of faith · the due praise of Martyrs · patience necessary ·
> persecution profitable

**Verdict: the 1635 Preface adds nothing and drops nothing.** It is the same text and the same apparatus,
reset in a wider measure — which is exactly why it occupies ten leaves where Douai needed twelve. Both
close identically: the subscription *"From the English College in Doway, the Octaues…"* followed by the
italic benediction.

## 5. The pattern, and the instances that break it

**The dominant movement is orthographic regularisation**, and it is consistent across every section:

- terminal **`-ie` → `-y`** (`holie`→`holy`, `manie`→`many`, `worthie`→`worthy`, `Citie`→`City`,
  `neceſſarie`→`neceſſary`, `happie`→`happy`, `iniquitie`→`iniquity`, `Seminarie`→`Seminary`)
- **`-e` dropped** (`booke`→`book`, `bookes`→`Books`, `briefe`→`brief`, `wordes`→`words`)
- **nouns capitalised** (`court`→`Court`, `goddes`→`Gods`, `age`→`Age`, `ſumme`→`Summe`)
- **`VV` → `W`** throughout, in display, prose and marginal notes alike
- one **outright correction**: `to large` → `too large`; and `leift in the margent` → `left in the margent`

**But it is not a modernisation programme, and saying so would be tidier than the evidence.** Several
changes run the other way, and they are not rare:

| 1609 | 1635 | |
|---|---|---|
| `AVTHOR` | `AVTHOVR` | *more* letters, more latinate |
| `authors` | `authours` | *more* letters |
| `Goſpel` | `Ghoſpel` | an `h` **added** |
| `dearly` | `dearely` | an `e` **added** |
| `S. Ierom` | `S. Hierom` | the `H` restored |
| `Doctors` | `Doctours` | *more* letters |
| `affayres` → `affaires` | but `Painimes` → `Paynimes` | **`ai`/`ay` swapped in both directions** |
| `tongues` | `tongs` | shortened |

The `ai`/`ay` pair is the clearest demonstration: the same digraph moves one way in one word and the
opposite way in another, in the same edition. **This is a resetting by a different house following its own
compositorial habits — not a programme.** Reporting it as "the 1635 modernises the spelling" would be a
generalisation the leaves do not support.

## 6. `VV` → `W` — the 1635 founts have a `W` sort, and the Douai founts do not

`GUIDELINES.md` §w-regime records that the display titling fount **has no `W` sort at all**, so display
matter is always `VV` and must be kept. **That is true of the 1582/1609 Douai-Rheims founts and false of
the 1635 Rouen founts.**

| | 1609 | 1635 |
|---|---|---|
| display | `VVELBELOVED` (leaf 13) | **`WELBELOVED`** — a single `W` sort (leaf 2) |
| marginal notes | `VVhy & how`, `VVhat part`, `VVhat is done` | `Why and how`, `What part`, `What is done` |
| italic closing | `tovvards` | `towards` |

And the 1609 prose is **itself mixed, on one page**, read on the base exemplar at ~545 ppi (leaf 13):
`we`, `which`, `wil`, `know`, `whom` set with a real `w`; **`vvorke` set as two sorts**; `VVherin` and
`VVherfore` with capital `VV`.

> **This corroborates Sir's ruling of 2026-08-06 directly, and on an admissible raster.** Mixed `w`, `vv`,
> `VV` and `Vv` are not an anomaly of one leaf — they occur **within a single page of the base exemplar**,
> and the availability of a `W` sort **differs between printing houses**. A global flip in either direction
> would erase a real distinction between the editions, and would do it silently. The rule is the one
> long-ſ already has: **read the glyph.** See roadmap R6.6, which withdraws a ratification made on `F`.

## 7. Errata — the 1635 carries its own, and it is not comparable

**1635 leaf 2070**: *`FAVLTS ESCAPED IN THE PRINTING.` / "The first word is the fault, the second the
correction." / `In the first Tome.`* — a dense list keyed by page, line and verse, running to a second
section headed `IN THE SECOND TOME.`

**This errata is keyed to the 1635's own pagination and records the 1635's own compositors' faults.** It is
therefore **not collatable line-by-line against the 1609's errata**: the two lists describe different
settings of type. What can be said is that the second edition **supplies an errata for itself**, correcting
its own printing rather than the first edition's — and that at least one first-edition error (`to large`)
was silently fixed in the text instead of being listed.

## 8. What this report does **not** cover

Stated plainly rather than left for a reader to discover:

| not collated | why, and what it would take |
|---|---|
| word-level body of the Preface beyond the opening period | the **adds/drops** question is closed by §3's note-sequence test; what remains is *rewording*, and it needs a full two-column read of 22 pages |
| bodies of the Summe, Proemial Annotations and the two Arguments | same — structure and headings are collated, bodies are not |
| the endmatter **Tables** (Controversies, *Hard Wordes*, etc.) | they sit at the far end of both volumes; `M`'s are near leaf 2068 |
| the **OT2 / 1610** prelims | `M`'s package holds both tomes; the second tome's prelims are further in and are not yet located |

**None of these blocks the edition.** `M` is admitted for prelims and endmatter only and supplies no OT
verse reading, so this collation is scholarly yield, not critical path — as the roadmap's own sequencing
note says.

## 9. What the collation establishes

1. **The 1635 is a faithful reprint of the Douai text.** Section for section, note for note, it neither
   adds nor drops. It even reproduces the 1609 approbation and its date.
2. **What Rouen changed is orthography and typography**, dominated by `-ie`→`-y`, dropped terminal `-e`,
   increased capitalisation and `VV`→`W`.
3. **The changes are not systematic in one direction**, and a report claiming they were would be false to
   the leaves.
4. **The `w`/`vv` availability differs by printing house**, which is a fact about the founts and a direct
   warning against global normalisation.
