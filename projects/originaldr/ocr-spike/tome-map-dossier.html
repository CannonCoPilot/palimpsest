The tome-map, end to end

What tome-map v1 is, what tome_prior did with it, what v2 replaced it with — and the defects found chasing it down. Rev 2 adds four more (the heading parser was reading half the corpus), retires M3 and M4, and corrects the headline accuracy figure a second time. Rev 3 runs the first book-grain four-way validation — Genesis — which found a reference that shifts part-way through a chapter, and then asks what a verse no witness can read actually looks like on the page.

Verdict

Your read is correct: the tome-map was incomplete and inaccurate — and v2 was too. v1 claims 3 books for a 765-page New Testament and 1 book for an 1,134-page Old Testament tome, and omits one admitted volume entirely.

And I owe you a correction on my own figure. The “100% held-out chapter accuracy” I have quoted for several turns was partly circular: the set it checked against already contained the printed heading being checked. Measured honestly, it was 61.5%. The gap turned out to be a broken heading parser, not broken addressing — with that and a second parser copy fixed, the honest figure became 94.4% — while the circular one sat at 100% throughout, which is exactly why it was worthless.

Rev 2: that 94.4% was itself measured on half the evidence, and it has been superseded. The heading parser was still only reading 2,372 of 4,640 heading-bearing pages, and the ones it missed were systematically the letter-spaced and degraded ones — the hard half. With the parser fixed the validation set grows to 3,861 labels and the honest figure is 86.95%. Lower, larger, and true. The circular metric was also still live in the persisted artifact, reading exactly 1.0 on all eleven volumes; it is now replaced and the old value kept under a name that forbids quoting it.

Why the prior was removed rather than re-pointed

v2 cannot be its own evidence

The circularity

v2 is derived from the addressing DP. Feeding it back as a prior would make the DP confirm its own previous answer — agreement that proves nothing, and that would then look like validation. A prior has to come from outside the thing it informs.

The correction

My accuracy figure was measuring itself

The addressing reports each page's chapter interval, chapters_on_page. I validated it by asking whether the chapter a page prints appears in that interval — but the interval is built partly from the printed heading. The check therefore asked whether the heading was in a set containing the heading. It answers 100% and always will.

The honest question is whether the addressing puts the page at the chapter the print says, using the DP's own position and nothing derived from headings. That was 61.5%, not 100%.

Why it was low — and it was not the addressing

Chapter headings are set letter-spaced, and the recognizer breaks the numeral up. My parser took the first whitespace-delimited token:

OCR linetrueparsed as
CHAP. X. V.XV = 1510
CHAP. X I.X.XIX = 1910
CHAP. X XII.XXII = 2210

The addressing placed those three pages at genesis 15, 18–19 and 22 — right every time. Every such page then carried a spurious “chapter 10”, which is what produced the bulk of the discontiguous-chapter errors below.

Defects found while writing this page

Broken — and fixed today

Three of these four were mine. All share one shape: a hand-typed list that nothing validated against the canon, which is the same shape as the three divergent LOCI dicts and the ground truth's 2john against the canon's 2-john.

D1

Zacharias was assigned to the wrong tome by a spelling typo fixed + guarded

I wrote zacharie in the OT2 book set; the canon spells it zacharias. Zacharias — a minor prophet, unambiguously in the second OT tome — was therefore classified OT1, given the OT1 state space, and could never be addressed because it is not physically in those volumes. It appeared as 13–14 missing chapters in three OT1 volumes and as an absent book in two more.

Fix: corrected, and the set now validates itself against the canon at import — an unrecognised slug raises rather than silently deleting a book. Zacharias is now mapped in all four OT2 volumes (14 chapters each).

D2

A second, drifting copy of the tome classification fixed

build_tome_map_v2.py carried its own hand-typed copy of the OT2 book set, which then disagreed with the corrected one — so OT2 volumes reported themselves as OT1+OT2. The fourth instance of this exact shape in the project.

Fix: the duplicate is deleted; there is now one classification, imported from the validated inventory.

D3

tome_prior fed v1 into the addressing disabled

A prior worth +0.4 per agreeing position, sourced from an artifact that is 0–25% accurate on three volumes. Disabled rather than re-pointed, for the circularity reason above, after measuring that it contributes nothing.

D4

v1's metadata was steering the addressing's state space superseded

v1 declares jp2-S04 — the 1633 Rheims New Testament — as covering ['NT','OT'], and gives jp2-S09ot2 nothing at all, which fell back to "both". A 27-book volume was handed a 73-book state space, and the addressing used the room: 140 pages of an OT volume placed on NT books, 772 in another.

Fix: the state space now comes from the declared witness inventory. Out-of-tome pages went from 6 of 11 volumes to 0 of 11.

D5

The heading pattern did not match how the Douay-Rheims prints headings fixed

The pattern was CHAP(?:TER)?, and it saw 2,372 of 4,640 heading-bearing pages — 51%. Three facts of the print defeated it, none of them rare: headings are set letter-spaced (C H A P. I.); the 1582/1610 New Testaments abbreviate to CHA., not CHAP., so archive-nt-1582 yielded six readable headings in 762 pages; and display capitals are misread in a small stable set (Cη, CN, O H, GHAP). A second guard compounded it — a display-line test counting whitespace tokens rejected C H A P. I. as "a sentence", so both defects failed on precisely the same pages.

Fix: one OCR-tolerant pattern in one module, plus a word count that collapses letter-spaced runs. The numeral is deliberately case-sensitive: with case folding, the marginal note Cη. Ad parsed as roman D — chapter 500. Precision was audited before the change: zero implausible chapter values corpus-wide, and a chapter-frequency distribution that decays monotonically from 1, which is what a genuine heading set looks like.

D6

The circular metric survived its own correction fixed

The circularity described above was corrected in the narrative but left live in the code, in the value written to every address artifact while the honest one went only to the console. It read exactly 1.0 on all eleven volumes, in both modes. The worst volume in the corpus was sitting at 52% underneath it.

Fix: the honest predicate — printed chapter against the addressing's own chapter — is now what is persisted. The old value is retained in the artifact under the name circular_accuracy_do_not_quote, so the file itself says why it is not the measure.

D7

A chapter's line range kept only the first printed-heading pin fixed

A chapter owns two pin segments whenever its heading falls mid-page — a carry-in run before it and a printed-heading run after. Taking only the second discarded everything above the heading: 3,006 pages and 67,284 body lines. jp2-S04 p680 is Apocalypse 1 across all 83 lines with CHA P. I. at line 44, so lines 0–43 were thrown away, and Apoc 1:11–15 went with them at a janvier fit of 0.79–0.98. The opposite failure ran alongside it: 275 pages whose chapter had only a carry-in pin were offered the whole page.

This predates the parser fix but was unreachable before it — there were almost no printed-heading pins to truncate on. Fixing recall upstream is what armed it. Fix: the range is the union of every pin naming the chapter. Mechanism proven by reconstruction: restoring the old parser reproduced the earlier attestation count exactly.

D8

Page furniture read as a chapter heading — three psalms erased fixed

jp2-S06 p1085, line 50 of 52, reads Pſal. 30 — below the last body line, after a catchword, beside the next leaf's T H E B O O K header. Read as a heading it is decisive evidence, so the page was addressed to Psalm 30; and because the addressing chain is monotone and cannot go back, pages 1086–1088 were dragged to 30 with it. Content alone had them right — 27, 28, 28, 29 — and p1086 plainly carries Vulgate Ps 28:6–7, "breake them in pieces as a calfe of Libanus … the voice of our Lord diuiding the flame of fire".

Monotonicity is what turns a 1.7% detection error into an erasure rather than a mislabel: one false forward jump makes every chapter in between unreachable. Fix: a heading must head something — there must be a body line after it on the page. 68 of 4,085 detected headings fail that test.

Still open

Missing — and how we fix it

M1

No apparatus or matter loci not built

You asked that the tome-map indicate whether a page maps to a book:chapter or apparatus. v2 carries only three page kinds — scripture, book-front-matter, no-scripture — and no matter locus at all. A front-matter table or a back-matter treatise is currently recorded as "no scripture here", which is true and useless: it cannot be looked up, scored, or routed to a re-OCR rung.

Fix: extend the address space with matter loci (matter/<tome>/<section>) drawn from the 31 curated matter ground-truth sections, and let the addressing DP place them the same way it places chapters — they are sequential blocks in the same volume, so the same monotone constraint applies. This also connects the matter work to the ladder, which you have asked for twice.

M2

No real PDF page reference not built

You asked for the map to reference both the jp2 and the correct page of each source's PDF. v2's source_page_index is just the OCR index copied across — not a PDF page, and for at least one volume not even the same numbering (jp2-S09ot2 is offset by one against its images).

Fix: add a per-volume PDF path and a verified PDF↔OCR offset, established the same way the jp2 offset was — render the PDF page, read it against the OCR text, record the shift. Cheap: one verification per volume, eleven in total.

M3

Matthew 1 unmapped in the NT volumes resolved 28 Jul

In jp2-S04 and pdf-S09nt the Matthew incipit page is addressed as book front matter, so chapter 1 never registers. Explainable and small, but it is a real hole at the head of the New Testament.

Resolved, and the diagnosis above was wrong. It was not a front-matter-boundary case. Two things were: the Preface to the Reader was being addressed as matthew 2, and — decisively — all eleven cached address files had been built with headings withheld, i.e. the held-out VALIDATION configuration was being used as the production deliverable basis. With headings restored as evidence and the parser fixed, all four NT volumes carry Matthew 1–28 complete at 100% coverage. Held-out runs are now kept separately, as measurements rather than as the product.

M4

v1 is still on disk and still read resolved 28 Jul

v1 remains the comparison baseline for the audit, which is legitimate — you cannot measure v2 against v1 without keeping v1. But it is also still imported by the addressing module for a fallback path that no longer fires.

The real liability was never the wasted read — a module-level json.loads makes the module fail to import the day the retired artifact is removed, so dead code that runs at import is not dead.

Rev 2 · the method that found D5–D8

Two stacks, validated together

Every defect on this page was found by something unrelated looking wrong. That is not a method. The audit now runs one book at a time, across every witness that carries it — which turns the witnesses into controls for one another and makes the diagnosis a single question.

The horizontal stack — parity

The six witnesses must reach the bar on the same verses. If one witness fails a verse its siblings read correctly, the defect is that volume's — its scan, its layout, its recognizer head. The parity spread, best minus worst on the same book, is the headline.

The vertical stack — layers

If every witness fails the same verse, no witness's recognizer is at fault. The defect is vertical, and it is attributed to the lowest layer that can explain it. Calling a truncated span "recognition" is exactly how D7 survived three rounds.

Rev 3 · four-way validation, then the failures

Genesis: what the witnesses say about each other

Before analysing failures, the four Genesis volumes were checked against one another. Structure is sound — 99.4–100% of 1,530 verses localized in every witness, no book attested by a volume that cannot hold it, no unreachable chapter. Four defects surfaced; one of them explains a chapter.

D9

The archaic reference shifts PART-WAY THROUGH a chapter fixed

Genesis 1 is aligned for 25 verses and then shifted by one for verses 26–31. Genesis 26 is shifted for 32 verses, and the offset itself grows from −1 to −2 part-way down. The existing realigner fits one offset per chapter and scores it over every verse, so an aligned head averages a shifted tail away and the chapter is declared sound — this is not a threshold that needed loosening, it is a model that could not represent the defect.

Fix: a monotone DP over offsets within the chapter — the same technique as the page addressing, one level down — where changing offset costs something, so the solver buys a second segment only when the text pays for it. Scored on archaic-vs-modern agreement alone, so no OCR can influence the alignment. 65 chapters corpus-wide, 474 loci inside the five audited books. Genesis 1's verses 26–31 went from being scored against their neighbours to 0.90–1.00 in all four witnesses; Genesis 26 chapter agreement 0.419 → 0.839.

D10

A one-verse reference stub overriding a complete one detected

s_dismas carries exactly one verse for Genesis 8 — verse 1 — and its text is actually Genesis 8:6–7. Because s_dismas is preeminent, that stub displaces odr_com's correct 8:1, so all four witnesses are scored against the wrong verse and cannot pass. Genesis 46 has the same shape.

Found by the four-way rule: when every independent copy agrees with the others and disagrees with the reference, it is the reference that is wrong.

D11

Volume front matter is addressed as Genesis 1 open

The Approbatio, the Epistle to the Reader and the Proemial Annotations land on chapter 1 — 24 pages in S1, 26 in S3, 27 in S9, 8 in S6. The damage is contained by the localizer's better-fit rule, which keeps 29–30 of 31 verses on real pages in S1 and S3, but S9 and S6 lose 12 and 11 Genesis-1 verses to front-matter pages.

Three fixes were tested and all three rejected. VOCABULARY fails because the front matter scores 0.32–0.51 against Genesis 1 — it is largely about Genesis. VERSE-NUMBER DENSITY fails because a threshold keeping 90% of real pages also keeps 51% of front matter. PEAKEDNESS fails worst: removing 89% of front matter also discards 57% of real scripture. The structural fix is M1 — front matter is forced into chapter 1 because the address space has nowhere else to put it. M1 is not a nice-to-have; it is the fix for this.

The finding

An all-fail verse is a BOUNDARY verse, not a hard verse. Every axis that separates the two groups is positional — opening a chapter, closing a page, straddling a page break. Every axis that measures difficulty — vocabulary, proper-name density, token length, marginal-apparatus load, column count — is flat. Genesis's residual failures are structural, and no recognizer improvement addresses them.

Three mechanisms, all at the chapter opening

  • The drop capital is detached and lost. Genesis 3:1 is "But the serpent"; every witness returns "but the serpent … beasts D of the earth" — the large initial emitted as a stray glyph mid-line while the verse itself begins lowercase.
  • The marginal note is spliced into the body. Genesis 9:1 returns "And he said to Ofthis com. IL them::: Increase … the earth. mandment, or I" — one note broken across two insertion points.
  • The chapter argument bleeds in. Two witnesses open Genesis 8:1 with "and offereth sacrifice" — the tail of the chapter summary, not scripture.

All three are line-construction artifacts: the recognizer reads the glyphs, and the line builder concatenates a drop cap, a marginal column and an argument line into the body stream where no downstream role filter can separate them.

Method

How any of this can be checked

  • Coverage is by construction: the addressing assigns every page of every admitted volume, so 100% is the floor, not an achievement. It is reported separately from accuracy because a run that addresses 90% of pages very accurately has not done the job.
  • Accuracy is held-out: printed chapter headings are removed from the evidence and then used as labels — but the label must be compared against the addressing's OWN position, never against a set the heading helped build. That mistake is what produced the discarded 100%. The live figure is 86.95% over 3,861 labels held-out, 93.94% in production mode where the headings are used as evidence.
  • Offsets are distinguished from errors by scoring every shift in −2…+2. A shifted index peaks sharply at one offset; a wrong claim is flat at all of them.
  • Regeneration is one command, and the audit that grades it is another: build_tome_map_v2.py then tome_map_audit.py.
