The safety result — measured, not asserted
Of the 23 verses the pipeline accepted, zero are worse vs gold than R2.

olmOCR is a high-variance rung: it rescues the median verse but fails catastrophically on a minority (the psalms two-column apparatus). The risk that raises — since acceptance is witness-based and the psalms witnesses are known-defective — is that the gate might “rescue” a verse with a reading that is actually worse. It does not. Every one of olmOCR’s catastrophic failures stayed OPEN; a reading bad enough to hurt gold also failed the witness bar. The gate over-escalates (safe) rather than over-accepts (unsafe). This is the No-Silent-Degradation guarantee, verified across 13 gold pages.

Section 1 · the gate

Confidence is blind; an independent witness is not

Recognizer confidence cannot see systematic misreads. Calibrated on 177 gold verses (46 known-bad), the cross-source alarm — R2’s per-verse reading vs an independent reference witness, janvier-cut — is the only signal that separates.

At recall = 1 (every known-bad flagged, per No Silent Degradation), the full gate escalates 33% of verses at τx=0.90 — versus 87% for confidence alone. The confident-wrong tail (42 verses with conf ≥ 0.92) is caught 42/42 by the cross-source alarm and 2/42 by the internal alarms. The external witness does essentially all the anti-laundering work — a router, never an acceptor.

Section 3 · the dual track

Content recovered is not diplomatically complete

olmOCR is a content rung. It modernizes the long-ſ on 80% of its outputs, so a verse whose content it rescues is not finished: the diplomatic surface is still owed. The pipeline scores the two axes separately and refuses to conflate them.

A naïve pipeline would report the content rescue and silently ship ſ-less text. This one holds the surface open.

≥τx · ſ keptRESCUED — accepted at R3
≥τx · ſ droppedRESCUED_CONTENT_S_OPEN — ſ owed → arbiter
<τx / no-geomOPEN — blocks deliverable

Genesis-24 makes it concrete: R3 lifted three verses’ content over the bar (24:27 0.88→1.00, 24:28 0.78→0.97, 24:29 0.88→0.99) — yet all three are held ſ-OPEN because olmOCR dropped the long-ſ, and two cross-page fragments stay OPEN. The ledger holds all five and blocks the deliverable, even though content was recovered. That is the point.

Section 4 · what remains

The next levers, in order

  • Layout-aware crop geometry for psalms — the biggest lever. The run localizes olmOCR’s weakness precisely (prose +0.11 vs psalms −0.31); a column-band crop per layout mode is the principled fix, and the harness already measures the exact metric to prove any improvement.
  • The ſ-faithful arbiter rung — close the 19 ſ-surface debts via the in-agent Jarvis arbiter reading the crop in-session (no API call), converting “content recovered” into “diplomatically complete”.
  • Hard cross-page fragments — verses whose text runs past the page need addressing/segmentation work, not a larger crop.
  • GT-3 breadth — the source-coverage transcription tail, best interleaved with review.

The rung is sound and measured: it genuinely rescues prose content, it never launders a worse reading, and its one real weakness is exposed, contained as OPEN, and has a clear next experiment. The pipeline reports its own limits rather than reporting success while degraded.
