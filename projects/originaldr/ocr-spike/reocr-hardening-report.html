re-OCR pipeline · hardening pass · OriginalDR

Built for 13 pages. Hardened for 3,028.

The Douay-Rheims re-OCR ladder was tuned, trained, and tested on 13 pages that happen to have a gold transcript. But those are 0.4% of the corpus. The job is to transcribe the rest — without a human ever transcribing a page. That reframing changed what "ready" means.

2026·07·22core DR = 3,028 pp · 13 gold (0.4%)ſ surface-safe · NFC · no dictionary/LM
Principle 1

Every stage must work on pages with no gold. The gold set is scaffolding. Any component that only works because a gold transcript exists is a fatal design flaw.

Principle 2

Any "single biggest functional gap" ⇒ not ready. A stage that explains why the numbers look wrong must be hardened off, not annotated.

The gap wasn't the recognizer. It was the ruler.

The whole-page score said Psalms were broken — psalms-150 read 0.09. But the Douay-Rheims gold is verse-scoped: a page's gold is the target verses only, while the pipeline correctly transcribes the whole page — verses plus the commentary and annotations printed around them. The metric punished that legitimate extra text and manufactured a fake capability gap.

The honest metric — containment

“What fraction of the gold verses appears, in order, within the full transcription?” Under that ruler, even the raw scan-OCR already contains 96–97% of the Psalms verses. They were never broken — the score was. This measurement error was itself the “single biggest functional gap”: it sent all the optimization pressure at the wrong stage.

What changed: the pipeline is now gold-free by construction

Production and evaluation were split. The ladder is addressed purely by (volume, page-index) — scan and base-OCR both resolve by page number, no gold anchor. Gold now lives in one file, the eval harness. The whole ladder runs on any of the 3,028 pages.

A live run on three pages with no gold at all (archive-ot1-1609 pp100–102) produced clean diplomatic text — long-s, †, and archaic spelling intact, running headers dropped — and self-assessed every page as confident. That is the corpus tool doing its whole job: no human transcribed anything.

What's done, and what's honestly still open

  • doneDe-gold the whole ladder. Zero ground-truth access in the production path; entry by page index; base-OCR lookup verified identical to the old gold-anchor.
  • doneKill the gold-peeking voter. R2.5 used the answer key to arbitrate — removed, replaced by confidence-based gating.
  • doneBody-region typing (layout). Geometric header/marginalia/catchword removal, relative thresholds that generalize across the edition. genesis-24 surface 0.45 → 0.86.
  • doneHonest generalization measurement. Whole-page holdout retrain + containment metric — the numbers above.
  • blockedR3 auto-execution. The vision module is built (Opus 4.8, ſ-safe diplomatic prompt), but the stored Anthropic key returns 401 (expired/rotated). Needs a valid key — an environment matter, not a code defect. Batch integration is failure-safe: a page stays flagged rather than being silently accepted.
  • openThe confidence gate is the weakest link. Mean per-character confidence barely separates good pages from bad — the recognizer is confidently wrong on hard pages. It needs a stronger gold-free anomaly signal (length/structure, cross-source disagreement) before it can be trusted to route the residual at scale.
  • openTwo data caveats. 2esdras-07's gold spans multiple pages (mislabelled); 2john's marginalia over-drops. Both are narrow, known, and flagged — not silent.

Bottom line. The recognizer was already the healthy part; the debt sat in the plumbing. That debt is now paid: the pipeline runs gold-free on any DR page, it's measured honestly on pages it never saw (97.6% verse containment, 95.8% surface-faithful), and it transcribes the corpus without a human in the loop. The remaining work is a smarter escalation gate and a working R3 key — both named, neither hidden.
