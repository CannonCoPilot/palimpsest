2 · What OriginalDR is

This is the original Douay-Rheims — Gregory Martin's translation for the English College (New Testament, Rheims 1582; Old Testament, Douay 1609–1610) — as distinguished from the later Challoner revision (gold idx 5, 100). It is a full Catholic Bible in the Clementine Vulgate canon: 76 books over two testaments plus a three-book appendix, 1,360 chapters, 8,378,088 characters of the edition's English in modern (ligature-expanded) orthography.

Unlike the marker/epub golds, OriginalDR is collated from three witnesses, each recorded per element in the map so any consumer can see exactly what supplied a given passage:

  • Madueke_A — the olprint “Augmented Bible” per-chapter HTML edition (codeberg) — supplies the authoritative scripture, the verse bodies of all 73 canonical books.
  • Sabates_A — the janvier-s/original-douay-rheims CC0 JSON dataset — supplies everything Madueke omits: the editorial apparatus (per-book arguments, per-chapter arguments, the 1,326 annotation blocks of footnotes and marginal commentary, and the 22 front/back reference documents) and the three-book apocryphal appendix (Prayer of Manasses, 3 & 4 Esdras).
  • Madueke_B — the merged-edition PDF of the same olprint project — a second format of the Madueke edition, used to confirm the HTML extraction is faithful (§5.2) and to corroborate the apparatus and the appendix (§6).
  • OCR-original-scan — third-party archive.org djvu OCR of the original 1582/1609/1610 printed editions — an independent print witness outside the Madueke/Sabates lineage, used to validate the digital text (§5.3).

The reconstruction places the apparatus at its canonical front/back positions and folds the per-chapter footnotes and cross-references in as masked annotation blocks after each chapter body. Because Sabates in fact derives from Madueke, the two are not fully independent — which is exactly why the third, print-based witness matters (§5.3). That breadth of both text and apparatus is what makes it a comprehensive gold rather than a bare scripture dump.

3 · The reconstruction process — generate, don't detect

Every other marker/epub gold in the set infers structure from text and can mis-anchor a mask. OriginalDR inverts that fragility. gen_dr_original.py walks the collated witnesses — Madueke scripture aligned verse-for-verse to Sabates apparatus — and emits the reference text paragraph by paragraph, recording each element's exact character offset as it writes it. The text and the map are the same object viewed two ways.

Why this matters
There is no detector to get wrong. A marker Bible's mask can drift if a chapter heading is formatted unexpectedly; here the offset was written by the same code that wrote the character. This is structurally the strongest guarantee available — and a preview of the "structure-first" import paradigm: when you already have the structure, perfect masks are free.

5 · Source collation & discrepancy analysis

Preferring one witness for the words is a provenance decision; proving the words are right needs collation. Three legs establish it, and the design is honest about what each can and cannot prove: leg 1 (a lineage-related digital witness) measures transcription fidelity, leg 2 (a second format of the same edition) measures extraction fidelity, and only leg 3 — an OCR of the original print outside that lineage — is a genuinely independent corroboration.

5.1 · Two-way digital collation — Madueke vs Sabates (leg 1)

A verse-by-verse string collation over the shared 35,809-verse canon, folding away orthographic and typographic convention to isolate genuine wording differences. Because Sabates derives from Madueke, this establishes transcription fidelity, not independence.

The only structural divergence is Tobias — Madueke 14 vs Sabates 15: Sabates carries a spurious leading chapter that Madueke does not, independently confirming the Tobias correction of §10. No verses are missing or extra on either side.

5.2 · Format-fidelity check — Madueke HTML vs merged PDF (leg 2)

Before trusting the HTML extraction, we confirm it against a second format of the same edition — the Madueke_B merged-edition PDF. This is an extraction-fidelity check, not independent corroboration.

Extraction is faithful — 100% type recall
All 14,099 distinct scripture word-types extracted from the Madueke_A HTML are attested in the 23,310-type merged edition (100.0% type recall, 100.0% token-weighted), with 0 missing types. The HTML parse loses no vocabulary relative to the PDF of the same edition — the two formats agree, so the extraction introduced no drift.

5.3 · Independent print-OCR validation (leg 3)

To break the shared-lineage risk, the digital text is collated against a genuinely independent witness: third-party archive.org djvu OCR of the original 1582/1609/1610 printed editions — outside both the Madueke/Sabates digital lineage and our own OCR pipeline. Rather than a handful of spot-checks, the sample is stratified-random: 36 chapters (seed 1729) drawn evenly across six text-type strata, with a 10,000-iteration chapter-resampled bootstrap giving a 95% confidence interval on recall.

Zero genuine discrepancies
Of 23,386 sampled tokens, 1,514 content words missed the skeleton-recall fold. After a second pass checking each against the whole print witness and the full merged edition, 0 remain as genuine candidates (0.0%) — every miss is OCR garble or a word attested elsewhere in the print. The independent print confirms both digital witnesses; shared-lineage agreement here is corroborated, not a shared error. The genuine-candidate filter is non-vacuous (a committed test proves it flags a truly-absent word and demotes a present one).

6 · Apparatus completeness

Scripture fidelity is not the same as apparatus completeness, so the two are measured separately. Across the 1,334 canonical chapters, Sabates supplies apparatus for 1,333 (99.925%) through five channels — book/chapter arguments, chapter summary notes, verse footnotes, cross-references and marginal sidecar notes. A chapter counts as covered if any channel is present, which is why the headline figure is near-total even though individual channels vary widely.

The sidecars are faithful, not truncated — corrects the earlier “Psalm 109 gap”
An earlier assessment read the empty marginal sidecars as lost transcription and singled out Psalm 109 as a gap. That was a measurement artifact: Psalm 109 in fact carries a summary, its verse-notes and cross-references — only its sidecar channel is empty. Corroboration settles it at the corpus level: the Madueke_B merged edition carries 406 annotation blocks against Sabates' 390 non-empty sidecars — a near-parity that shows Sabates' marginal coverage tracks the print, and the 940 chapters without a sidecar are chapters the print did not annotate marginally, not data that was lost.
The appendix is now two-witness
The three-book apocryphal appendix (Prayer of Manasses, 3 & 4 Esdras) — previously Sabates-only — is located in the Madueke_B merged edition at recorded character offsets, so it is corroborated by a second witness rather than resting on a single source.
One genuine gap — documented, not filled
Exactly 1 of 1,334 chapters has no apparatus in any Sabates channel: 2 Paralipomenon 12. It is a faithful bare-heading chapter — no apparatus appears in any digital witness — so it is documented rather than fabricated from the column-flattened Madueke_B (which cannot be reliably parsed at the sub-chapter level).

10 · The Tobias correction

Tobias — restored to 14 chapters
The upstream CC0 dataset carried a spurious leading “chapter 0” for Tobias: a single verse holding only a truncated 8-word fragment of the book's opening line (“Tobias of the tribe and city of Nephthali”). The complete verse — and the whole real first chapter — follows immediately after, so the enriched reconstruction drops the fragment and renumbers, restoring the canonical 14 chapters the Vulgate expects.

The drop is verifiably lossless: source chapter 0 holds one 8-word verse, while source chapter 1 opens with that exact text in full (“…Nephthali, which is in the upper parts of Galilee…”) and runs a further 25 verses. Nothing is discarded — only a parsing artifact is removed. A scan of all 77 source books found Tobias to be the only book with this thin-leading-chapter signature, so the fix is a targeted one-book correction (SPURIOUS_LEADING_CHAPTER in the generator), not a systemic re-parse. The leg-1 collation (§5.1) independently confirms it: Tobias — Madueke 14 vs Sabates 15, with the extra chapter present only on the Sabates side.

The 14-chapter count is independently corroborated by the book's own Argument (preserved as the masked introduction apparatus), which divides Tobias “The first four chapters… The eight following… In the two last chapters” — 4 + 8 + 2 = 14. With the count restored, the sources manifest carries no canon_exceptions and the Catholic oracle passes all 76/76 books with zero discrepancies.

12 · Limitations & what is not proven

  • Apparatus wording is single-transcription. Coverage is near-complete (99.925%) and corroborated by Madueke_B at the count level, and the appendix is two-witness — but the apparatus text itself is not yet collated word-for-word against a second witness the way scripture is. One chapter (2 Paralipomenon 12) carries no apparatus in any digital witness.
  • Print validation is a stratified sample. The independent OCR covered 36 chapters across six text-type strata with a bootstrap CI — enough to support a statistical claim, but not an exhaustive verse-by-verse print collation of all 1,334 chapters. Recall is also a lossy skeleton-fold signal; the genuine guarantee is the 0-candidate distinctive-word miss count, not the recall percentage itself.
  • Chapter-granular oracle. The Catholic oracle checks book presence, order and chapter count — not verse counts or verse text. Vulgate verse divisions vary by edition, so verse counts are recorded, not gated.
  • One deliberate editorial correction. The reconstruction departs from raw-source WYSIWYG in exactly one place — the spurious Tobias chapter is dropped (§10), a fix independently confirmed by the Madueke witness, which never carried it.
